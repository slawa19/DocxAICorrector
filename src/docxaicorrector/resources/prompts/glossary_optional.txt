You prepare the glossary of ONE book before it is translated block by block from {source_language} into {target_language}.
Every block is translated by a separate call that cannot see the others, so any term or name that is not fixed here may be rendered differently in different chapters. Your glossary is added to every one of those calls.

The glossary already holds the expressions the author introduced, listed in "already_fixed" with their renderings — do not repeat them, and do not give any other candidate a rendering that one of them already has.

The user message is a JSON object with:
- "candidates": expressions found in the book by their form, each with how often it occurs, the signals that found it and one short context. Signals: "capitalised" (a name), "heading" (a word or phrase of a chapter title that the text keeps using), "collocation" (a recurring word combination), "family_head" (a word shared by several candidates), "emphasis" or "quoted" (marked once by the author), "phrase" (a recurring expression built on a term of the glossary: it needs its own rendering, not one assembled from the word it contains);
- "already_fixed": the renderings already in the glossary;
- "max_terms": the largest number of entries you may return.

Candidates with "required": true were left out by an earlier answer and are in the glossary already: return a rendering for every one of them.
From the other candidates, choose the entries whose rendering must stay the same throughout the book, most important first:
- the book's own concepts and terms of art, especially the subjects named in its chapter titles, and those a translator could plausibly render in more than one way;
- proper names of people, places, organisations, currencies, programmes and publications — one spelling or transliteration each, following established {target_language} usage where one exists;
- skip ordinary vocabulary that has one obvious translation, fragments that are not expressions of their own, and artefacts of text extraction (split words, stray letters).
Return as many as the candidates justify, at most {max_terms} besides the required ones.

Give each entry the single rendering to use everywhere, in the dictionary form of {target_language} (it will be inflected in context). If the book clearly uses one candidate in two distinct senses, you may return it twice with a different "note" each time.
For every entry of kind "name": transliterate by the standard practical transcription of {target_language} (the system its publishers and reference works use, not a letter-by-letter rendering of the spelling), and keep the full form the author uses — the first name together with the surname when the book gives both; the source_term stays exactly as given.

Answer with JSON only, no commentary, in exactly this shape:
{{"terms": [{{"source_term": "<a candidate, exactly as given>", "target_term": "<rendering>", "kind": "term" or "name", "note": "<empty, or when this rendering applies>"}}]}}
