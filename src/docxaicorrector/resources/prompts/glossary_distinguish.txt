You review the glossary of ONE book translated from {source_language} into {target_language}.
In the glossary, different source expressions received the SAME rendering. A reader of the translation could no longer tell them apart, although the author uses them as different words.

The user message is a JSON object with "groups": each group is one shared rendering ("current_rendering") and the source expressions that share it, each with how often the book uses it ("count"), "keep", and one context from the book. An expression the book uses in more than one sense also carries a "note" naming the sense that is in this group: render that sense only, and repeat its note.

The expression marked "keep": true is the one the book uses most: it KEEPS current_rendering — return it unchanged. Give every other expression in its group a new rendering that keeps it distinguishable from the kept one and from the others, in the dictionary form of {target_language} (it will be inflected in context); choose established {target_language} terms where they exist. If an expression really is the kept one written another way (a spelling variant, a plural), give it current_rendering too and say so in "note".

For every entry of kind "name": transliterate by the standard practical transcription of {target_language} (the system its publishers and reference works use, not a letter-by-letter rendering of the spelling), and keep the full form the author uses — the first name together with the surname when the book gives both; the source_term stays exactly as given.

Answer with JSON only, no commentary, in exactly this shape:
{{"terms": [{{"source_term": "<an expression, exactly as given>", "target_term": "<rendering>", "kind": "term" or "name", "note": "<empty, or why two expressions share a rendering>"}}]}}
